The bootstrap sweep established the regulatory picture well and the commercial picture
barely at all. Ranked by decision impact:

**1. Can a foreign principal be the independent validator of record in Japan?**
If independence has to be a domestic legal entity, the vehicle question is answered for us
and the 顧問 becomes a co-principal rather than an advisor. Everything downstream depends
on this. *Route: Japanese-language procurement material; a lawyer; the ex-CRO archetype.*

**2. What does an engagement cost, and how is it procured?**
Consulting project, audit engagement, or outsourced function? Per-model or per-year? Until
we know this there is no business model, only a thesis. *Route: Finalyse and LSEG service
structures as comparables; buyer conversations.*

**3. Is the AI validation gap real or already crowded?**
`note-ai-validation-gap` is the whole near-term strategy and it rests on an untested
inference. *Route: check whether Big 4 Japan have launched AI model validation offerings
since March 2025; survey JRMV and arXiv for existing methodology papers.*

**4. Which regional banks are actually exposed?**
The beachhead segment is currently a BoJ aggregate statistic, not a list of names.
*Route: disclosure documents; the ex-CRO archetype; regional bank IT vendor relationships.*

**5. What does TUJ permit?**
Decides whether the academic-lab vehicle is real. *Route: one conversation, cheap, do it early.*

**6. Who are the named 顧問 candidates?**
Deliberately last. Names without a decided profile and a publication base to show them are
wasted approaches. *Route: JAFEE officer lists and conference programmes.*

**Note on method.** Questions 1, 2 and 4 cannot be answered by desk research. The plan's
real bottleneck is access to people in the buyer's seat — which is why the advisor stream
sits on the critical path rather than beside it.
