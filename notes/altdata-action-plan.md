Four steps, in order. Nothing else in this space matters until these are done.

## 1. Build the harness on free data — by end November

Data integrity checks, signal construction with multiple-testing correction, orthogonalisation
against standard factors, backtest **with and without** the dataset, shock and regime analysis,
capacity and costs. Output is a fixed report template. J-Quants supplies the market data.
Weekends, not months.

## 2. Publish one worked example — by end December

Run it on a public dataset, publish the result including the negatives, with the academic
affiliation on the byline. Choose a **non-equity or Japanese** dataset, where the platform
competition does not reach.

## 3. Take it to three vendors — by end January

Ask what proving their data costs them today, and whether a desk would discount a report they
paid for. Start with Nowcast.

## 4. Decide the unit of sale — after, not before

## Why this order, and not the other one

Everything here is sequenced so that **the first two steps need no client, no introduction, no
entity and no permission.** That is deliberate. The previous venture stalled because every path
ran through someone who had to agree first — a 顧問, a buyer, a regulator's view of
independence. This one starts with work you can do alone, on free data, and the output is what
makes step three a demonstration instead of a pitch.

It also front-loads the honest test. If building the harness is not enjoyable and the published
example is not good, the answer arrives in two months at a cost of some weekends, rather than
in two years at the cost of a business.

## What to ignore for now

- Distribution. `org-battlefin` already runs the meetings; be the researcher whose report gets
  brought to one, not a rival channel.
- Market sizing. The forecasts are junk (`note-altdata-market-size`) and the demand surveys
  already say enough.
- Japan as a standalone market. It is the differentiator on the way to selling to global
  vendors, not the market itself.
- The other venture. It is parked, not deleted.

## The one thing that would change this plan

If step three finds that vendors already pay `org-exabel` and are satisfied, the gap is
narrower than it looks and the right move is to sell **into** that ecosystem — the bespoke,
non-equity, incremental-value work a platform cannot do — rather than beside it. That is not a
failure; it is a smaller and more defensible position, reached in three months instead of
guessed at now.
