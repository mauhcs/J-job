## Tier 1 — Big 4, productised and already local

KPMG Japan runs a named Japanese-language MRM practice. PwC Japan sells model governance
態勢 construction out of its Assurance/GRC practice. Deloitte states the widest scope
globally. EY is automating the work via its BPM platform.

The shape of their offer is consistent: **governance, documentation, inventories,
frameworks, and validation of standard credit and market risk model classes.** Sold to the
second line of defence, priced as a consulting programme.

## Tier 2 — domestic think-tank consultancies

NRI and ABeam have deep incumbency with Japanese financial institutions. Neither was
confirmed to run a productised model validation line in the first sweep. Where model risk
does appear in Japanese-language material from ABeam and Accenture Japan, it is framed
around **AML/CFT transaction monitoring** — which suggests that is the frame the Japanese
market reaches for by default.

## Tier 3 — specialist boutiques

Finalyse (independent valuation of structured products and complex OTC derivatives, as a
fully outsourced managed service), Kevin D. Oden & Associates, HedgeStar, LSEG's model
validation and benchmarking service. All Western. **None was found operating in Japan.**

## Correction, 2026-09-22

Tier 3 was wrong. Fintech協会's venture member list carries a dedicated **ALM・リスク管理**
category containing five Japanese firms — KYCコンサルティング, コンプライアンス・データラボ,
ステラリスクコンサルティング, rubi labs, 株式会社レンズ — plus EduCare under スコアリング. See
`org-jp-risk-boutiques`.

Whether they do *independent model validation* or ALM advisory, compliance data work and
implementation is not yet established, and that distinction decides whether they are
competitors or partners. The likelier reading is partner: domestic contracting, language and
relationships on their side, deep quantitative modelling on ours.

The gap below is therefore stated with less confidence than when it was written, and
`task-classify-jp-risk-boutiques` exists to settle it.

## The gap, stated plainly

Tier 1 owns compliance and governance. Tier 3 owns deep quantitative validation — but
operates in Europe and the US. In Japan and Hong Kong, a buyer who needs genuine
quantitative teardown of a specific model, rather than a governance programme, has no
obvious local specialist to call.

That is the thesis. Three things would falsify it and none has been checked yet: that the
Big 4 Japan quant benches are in fact deep; that domestic buyers do not distinguish
governance from technical validation and will not pay separately for the latter; or that a
Tier 3 firm is already in the market unnoticed. **Checking these is the highest-priority
open work in the market stream.**
