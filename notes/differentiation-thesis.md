A hypothesis, not a finding. It needs to be tested against real buyers.

**The claim.** Big 4 model validation is staffed by risk consultants who learned validation
as a compliance discipline. The scarce profile is someone who has *built and traded* the
models — who reads a pricing or execution model the way its author does and can say what
will actually break, rather than whether the documentation is complete.

**The three components, and what each is worth**

1. **HFT and quant trading.** Credible on microstructure, execution, latency-sensitive
   pricing and the failure modes of models under live conditions. Very thin on Big 4
   benches. Sells to securities firms, funds, and bank trading desks.
2. **Risk modelling.** The entry ticket. Without it, item 1 reads as "trader", not
   "validator", and nothing clears a second line of defence.
3. **On-chain rate models (IPOR).** Genuinely scarce — validation methodology for on-chain
   rate and oracle models barely exists. Publishable, and directly relevant to Hong Kong's
   licensed digital-asset venues. Also the component most likely to be misread as
   unserious by a conservative Japanese bank.
4. **Academic affiliation (TUJ).** What makes *independent* mean something, and what makes
   the publishing and 顧問 approaches natural rather than transactional.

**The synthesis to test:** "independent verification of quantitative models by someone who
has built them in production, with an academic base." Not "cheaper Big 4."

**How to test it.** Not by more desk research. By five conversations with people who sit in
the buyer's seat — which is precisely what the ex-CRO archetype unlocks, and why the
advisor stream is on the critical path for positioning, not parallel to it.

## Open questions

- Does a Japanese second line of defence value production experience, or read it as a
  conflict of interest?
- How is crypto sequenced — lead in HK, footnote in Japan? Or kept off the Japanese
  material entirely?
