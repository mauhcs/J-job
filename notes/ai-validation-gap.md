Three regulators across three jurisdictions have, within eighteen months, required
independent validation of AI-driven models without specifying how to do it.

- **MAS** — consultation closed 31 January 2026; final guidelines expected mid-2026 with a
  **12-month compliance window**. Higher-risk AI uses (credit decisions, risk management,
  customer-facing advice) require independent validation, stress testing, transparency and
  human oversight, with validation by teams independent of development.
- **JFSA** — AI Discussion Paper v1.0 (March 2025) and v1.1 (March 2026); reported
  explainability expectations for AI-driven credit scoring.
- **BoJ** — August 2026 survey: governance, third-party risk management and safety/security
  identified as the weak areas; about half of institutions report difficulty with
  training-data sufficiency and quality control.

**Why this is the opening.** A compliance deadline creates a dated budget line. The
methodology does not exist, so incumbency counts for less than usual — a Big 4 bench built
on IFRS 9 and credit rating validation has no more of an answer here than anyone else.
And the required skill is not audit: it is understanding non-stationary statistical models
well enough to say whether one is fit for the decision it is making. That is the actual
overlap with an HFT and quant-trading background.

**What follows.** Lead with AI model validation across all three geographies; treat
traditional pricing and market-risk validation as the credential that makes it credible
rather than as the headline offer. The flagship paper should be on this.

**Caveat.** `confidence: med` — the regulatory facts are solid, but the inference that this
is commercially open rather than already crowded is untested. Checking it is the first job
of the next research cycle.
