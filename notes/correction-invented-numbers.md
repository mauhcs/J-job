Recorded so the error stays visible rather than being quietly edited away.

**What was written.** `note-unit-economics` carried a table of engagement fees by tier —
"Startup: 4–8 days, ¥1.5–3m", "SME: 8–15 days, ¥3–6m", and so on — and `note-feasible-play`
built a revenue model on top of it.

**What was wrong with it.** The fees were a triangulated day rate multiplied by a guessed day
count. **Both numbers were invented.** No published pricing exists for model validation
engagements in Japan, and no effort data was found either — so neither factor had a source.
Multiplying two unsourced numbers produced a precise-looking figure with nothing behind it.

**The specific objection that lands.** A seed-stage lender does not pay ¥3m for a report.
And "4–8 days" is not a real estimate: understanding someone else's model well enough to have
a defensible opinion on it can take weeks on its own, before any testing. The effort estimate
was low by an unknown but probably large factor, which means the startup tier's economics were
not merely optimistic — they were the wrong sign. Cost to deliver likely exceeds what that
tier can pay.

**The deeper error.** The SME wedge rested on an inference: that warehouse lenders,
securitisation investors, bank partners and VCs demand independent model review. **Not one
instance of a Japanese fintech actually being asked for one was ever found.** That was
correctly flagged as an open question, and then a strategy, a tier model and a revenue
projection were built on top of it as though it were settled. Marking something as unverified
does not license using it as a premise.

**What stands and what does not.**

- Stands: the regulatory picture (JFSA, HKMA, MAS, BoJ — all sourced and quoted), the named
  company lists, the ten-fold gap between contractor and expert rates, and the utilisation
  benchmarks for solo consultants.
- Does not stand: every engagement fee, every day count, the tiered revenue model, and the
  claim that demand exists at the startup tier.

See `note-deliverable-spec` for what the work actually consists of, and `note-sales-routes`
for the part none of this addressed.
