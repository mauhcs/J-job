`note-game-regulation` is the most differentiated idea in this project and it rests on an
untested assumption: that studios feel exposed on the accuracy of disclosed drop rates.

Establish:

- Has the **消費者庁** ever acted over a *misstated probability*, as opposed to the mechanic
  itself? Review enforcement history and the CEDEC 2024 session on past cases.
- Have players ever publicly demonstrated a discrepancy between disclosed and observed rates,
  and what happened to the studio when they did? Player-run rate tracking is common — a
  documented incident is the entire sales case.
- What do the JOGA and CESA guidelines require operators to be able to **evidence**, rather
  than merely display?
- Does any law firm, auditor or QA vendor already offer this?

**If enforcement has only ever targeted mechanics, the perceived risk is near zero and this
idea is a solution to a problem nobody feels.** That verdict is worth reaching quickly, because
it is the one idea here that no competitor is positioned to take.
