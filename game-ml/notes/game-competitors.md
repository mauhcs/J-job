Japan has a **mature, well-capitalised AI contracting sector**, and it is not a gap market.

**Research-led ventures:** Preferred Networks (develops its own MN-Core AI chip), ABEJA,
Exawizards, PKSHA Technology, AI inside, FRONTEO, **HEROZ**.

**Custom AI contractors:** Laboro.AI (founded 2016, listed on TSE Growth in 2023), Ridge-i,
AVILEN, BrainPad. These sell "custom AI" — bespoke design and build against a client problem,
including small and mid-sized projects.

**The one to look at first is `org-heroz`**, whose origins are in shogi AI and which works in
both game AI and finance. That is the same combination this project proposes, already
assembled in a domestic listed company with Japanese client relationships.

## How to read this

**Competing head-on is not viable.** They have Japanese-language sales, existing studio
relationships, delivery teams, and in Laboro's case public-company credibility. A solo
foreign consultant does not win a competitive tender against them.

**But contracting sectors this deep subcontract.** A bench organised around CV, NLP and
generative work needs outside help when a brief is adversarial detection, economic modelling
or regulated-probability verification. That is the same structural conclusion the other two
projects reached — Big 4 for validation, Neudata for data — arrived at a third time
independently.

**The unresolved question:** do these firms even get asked for this kind of work by game
studios, or does it never reach them because studios handle it internally? That decides whether
subcontracting is a channel or a dead end (`task-game-approach-vendors`).
