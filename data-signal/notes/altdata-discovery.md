## The honest answer on Google Ads

Probably a waste, for three structural reasons.

1. **Almost nobody searches for this.** The buyer is a data vendor's founder or head of
   sales. They do not type "independent dataset alpha evaluation" into Google, because the
   category has no settled name. Search advertising harvests existing demand; there is barely
   any to harvest.
2. **The incumbents already own the queries that do exist.** The high-intent vendor searches —
   *how to sell data to hedge funds*, *data monetisation* — return Neudata's and Eagle Alpha's
   own guides at the top. They have spent years building that content. Buying your way past it
   at auction, in a niche with tiny volume and long sales cycles, is expensive and slow.
3. **This industry does not run on search.** It runs on a newsletter, a podcast, three brokers
   and a handful of conferences. Everyone in it reads the same things.

**The one genuinely useful keyword exercise:** not to buy ads, but to *find the questions
vendors actually ask*. Run keyword research on phrases like "how do I prove my dataset has
alpha", "does my data have alpha", "dataset backtest for hedge funds". Anything with real
volume and thin competition is a piece of content worth writing, and it will earn a position
that an ad only rents. Treat the keyword tools as research into vendor anxiety, not as a
media buy.

| **LinkedIn** | Where the community actually converses | Post the work, not promotion |

**One podcast episode reaches buyers, sellers and brokers simultaneously.** That is the whole
inbound problem solved in a single move, and it costs nothing but having something to say.

## Six ideas for being discovered, ranked by fit

1. **A public dataset teardown series.** One dataset a month, same method every time, negatives
   published. This is the strongest idea in the note: it is the marketing, the credential, the
   portfolio and the product demo in one artefact, it compounds, and **nobody else publishes
   honest negative evaluations** because every existing publisher is paid by a vendor. Being
   the person who says "this dataset does not work, here is the evidence" is a position no
   incumbent can occupy.
2. **Open-source the evaluation harness on GitHub.** Quants find tools through code. A
   well-built repo is a permanent credential that works while you sleep, and it converts
   "trust me" into "read it".
3. **Answer the question vendors actually ask, definitively.** One canonical piece: *how do you
   prove a dataset has alpha?* — with the method, the pitfalls, the multiple-testing trap.
   This is what earns newsletter links and search position simultaneously.
4. **Own the Japan niche in English.** "Evaluating Japanese alternative data" is uncontested.
   Small audience, zero competition, and it makes you the obvious call for a category of work
   the incumbents turn down.
5. **Get on the podcast** (see above).
6. **Directory listings.** Low effort, low yield, do it once.

## What to ignore

Paid search, cold email, and anything that scales — this is a market of perhaps a few hundred
relevant firms where everyone reads the same newsletter. **Being known by two hundred of the
right people is complete market coverage**, and that is a publishing problem, not an
advertising one.
