Where this stands

    1. Regulation is the demand engine, and it has fired twice. JFSA's 2021 Principles built internal teams at the top of the market; the 2025–26 AI guidance from JFSA, MAS and the BoJ created a second wave at institutions that never built one. This part is sourced and quotable.
    2. Hong Kong gives explicit permission. HKMA SPM CA-G-4 states independent review may be performed by external parties with relevant experience, and the eight licensed digital banks are the best-defined target set in the plan.
    3. Demand at the SME tier is unverified. The wedge assumed warehouse lenders, securitisation investors and VCs ask for independent model review. No instance of a Japanese fintech actually being asked has been found. Treat it as a hypothesis.
    4. The engagement fees were withdrawn. They were a triangulated day rate times a guessed day count. Effort is the missing factor and no published data on validation duration exists — so it has to be observed before anything is quoted.
    5. Positioning is worth roughly ten times the rate. A quantitative risk contractor bills £215/day; a niche independent expert bills $300–500/hour for the same skill. That gap is real and sourced, and it is what the academic affiliation and publishing buy.
    6. Sales is the binding constraint, so start where no selling is required. Deliver for firms that already hold the mandates — Big 4 subcontractor panels, domestic risk boutiques — and take inbound from expert networks.
    7. Companies do buy research, and it is sized like a marketing budget. ¥97.7bn a year flows from Japanese companies into university joint research, average contract ¥3.3m, 85% under ¥3m. That buyer is marketing or the CEO, not risk or procurement — and the work is the publication record, paid for. Blocked on whether TUJ can sign a 共同研究契約.
    8. Sell in this order: paid research, then model building, then validation. The plan started at validation, which is the one that needs a credential, independence and a demand premise none of which existed yet.
    9. What a startup buys from a university is a name, grant access and capability — not scrutiny. The Japanese literature is blunt that a professor's name is indispensable when a company competes on technical strength, and that partnering unlocks subsidies a company cannot reach alone.
    10. Monetise the credential, not a consultancy. Expert-witness work, benchmark oversight under IOSCO Principle 5, an outside-director seat, and paid teaching through きんざい all share the property this plan needs: the counterparty initiates.

    Work upward. At the bottom the purchase is forced by warehouse lenders, securitisation investors and VCs in diligence — decisions made in weeks. At the top it is forced by the supervisor — decisions made in quarters. Each tier's reference case is what makes the next one possible.
