## What the CV says

Chief Scientist at IPOR Labs (on-chain interest rate benchmarks); Adjunct Assistant Professor
at Temple University Japan; background in HFT, quant trading and risk modelling. Based in
Japan.

## Where that is genuinely strong

**DeFi quantitative risk.** The rarest thing on the CV. Very few people have built on-chain
rate benchmarks *and* have traditional risk modelling behind them. Firms doing quantitative
research on protocol economics and market risk — Gauntlet is the clearest example — are
looking for exactly this combination, and it is largely remote, which removes the geography
constraint entirely.

**Model risk in Japan.** An unusual overlap: quantitative depth, an academic post, resident in
Japan, and — thanks to the `model-validation` project — current knowledge of JFSA's Principles,
the AI Discussion Papers, HKMA CA-G-4 and the MAS timetable. That research was done for a
business that did not work; as interview preparation it is worth a great deal.

**Anything requiring both production trading systems and statistical rigour.** HFT experience
signals that models were run with money behind them, which reads differently from a research-
only background.

## Where it is weak

**Japanese language for business use.** Tokyo risk and quant roles routinely specify bilingual.
This is the single largest filter on the domestic market and no amount of technical fit
substitutes for it.

**Buy-side pedigree.** Global funds hiring in Japan want strong views on FX, rates, credit or
equity markets and often a PhD. HFT is adjacent to, not the same as, discretionary or
systematic macro research.

**Recency in traditional finance.** The current role is crypto-adjacent and the academic post
is part-time. Some banks read that as distance from the field.

**Game and applied ML.** No track record, per the `game-ml` project. Not a realistic job
market, only a services idea.

## The honest ranking

1. **DeFi / protocol risk**, remote — best fit, least constrained by language or location.
2. **Senior model risk in Japan**, at a global bank's Tokyo office or a Japanese group building
   the function — if the language bar can be cleared or the role is English-operating.
3. **Academic**, via JREC-IN — a known quantity, and compatible with everything else.
4. **Quant research at a fund** — plausible but competitive, and the market wants a different
   specialism.
5. **Applied ML in games** — not a job market for this profile.
