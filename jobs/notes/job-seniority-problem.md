Worth stating before any list of postings is taken seriously.

The model-risk and quant roles advertised in Tokyo are largely **Associate and VP level**.
Nomura's Risk Management department, for instance, advertises a Model Validation Quantitative
Analyst at **Associate** grade. Typical requirements run to a master's in a STEM field and
professional experience in Python, R or VBA.

For someone holding a Chief Scientist title and a university post, that is a step down in
seniority and, very likely, in pay.

## Why this matters for how to search

**Senior roles are rarely advertised.** Head-of-function, lead quant and principal researcher
positions are filled through executive search and personal networks, which is why the public
boards skew junior. Scanning job boards therefore samples the wrong end of the market.

Three implications:

1. **Use the boards as market intelligence, not as the application route.** A posting reveals
   that an institution is building a model risk function — which is useful even when that
   specific role is wrong.
2. **The recruiters matter more than the postings.** Morgan McKinley, Robert Walters and Selby
   Jennings all place senior risk and quant roles in Tokyo; a conversation with a specialist
   recruiter reaches the unadvertised market that a board search cannot.
3. **Remote DeFi and crypto roles are the exception.** That market advertises openly at senior
   level and does not run on Tokyo's relationship networks.

## The reframe worth considering

A job search and the consulting ideas in the other projects are not separate activities.
A senior in-house role would supply the exact thing every venture in this repo lacked — a
track record, a network, and a reference — while paying for the time. Taking a role is the
cheapest available solution to the constraint that killed three business ideas.
