## Volume, by search

Result counts on the boards, as a rough measure of market depth:

| search | count |
|---|---|
| Model risk — Singapore (eFinancialCareers) | **465** |
| Quantitative — Hong Kong (eFinancialCareers) | **312** |
| Quantitative — web3.career, all remote/crypto | **235** |
| Risk manager — Japan (eFinancialCareers) | **189** |
| Morgan McKinley Japan, all roles | **364** |
| Quant analyst — Tokyo (eFinancialCareers) | **22** |

These are keyword matches, not curated fits — the Japan "risk manager" results were mostly
sales, operations and compliance roles rather than quantitative ones. Treat them as an
indication of depth, not of opportunity.

**The pattern is unambiguous: Singapore and Hong Kong are deeper markets than Tokyo for this
profile**, by an order of magnitude on quantitative risk. That is the reverse of where the
`model-validation` project spent most of its effort.

**Correction:** the Tokyo ¥14M–17M credit risk model developer figure came from a recruiter
advert with no named employer, and that card has been deleted per
`note-job-verification-standard`. The number is retained here as a market signal only — it is
an advertised band, not a verified role.

## What the numbers say

**Hong Kong publishes senior compensation; Tokyo largely does not.** Hong Kong advertises
Director, Head-of and Lead roles with bands attached. Tokyo advertises Associate roles with
"competitive salary". That is a structural difference in how the two markets hire, and it
means a Tokyo-only search systematically understates what is available.

**Crypto pays the most and varies the most.** A $250k–350k band and an $87k–110k band sit on
the same job board under similar titles. Compensation there tracks the firm, not the role —
so judge each employer individually and never use an aggregate.

**Tokyo at ¥14M–17M for a senior model developer** is a genuine number and not a bad one — it
simply appears far less often than the Associate postings that dominate the listings.
