## Question

What exactly is sold, to whom, for how much — and is it defensible against the platforms that
already do a version of it?

## The proposition being tested

*"Give me access to your data and I will find something meaningful"* — sold to the **data
owner**, not the fund. The deliverable is a research report a vendor can put in front of a
trading desk as the reason to buy.

With a methodological core that is the actual product: **run strategies over history with and
without the dataset**, with risk parameters and shock analysis, to scope the dataset's
*incremental* value rather than its standalone appeal.

## Why the buyer is the data owner

Because it is a **pre-sales and marketing spend**, not a research spend. A vendor with an
unproven dataset cannot get a fund to spend evaluation resources on it; a credible independent
report removes that objection. The same logic that made research-as-marketing work in the
other space applies here, with a much better profile fit.

## The uncomfortable part, up front

**A version of this already exists as a product** — see `org-exabel`. The question is not
whether the idea is good but whether there is room beside a funded platform. See
`note-altdata-offer-design`.

## Open questions

- Do data vendors actually pay for this today, and who do they pay?
- What does a vendor's pre-sales budget look like?
- Is "incremental value against an existing book" a deliverable buyers recognise, or a
  methodology only quants care about?
