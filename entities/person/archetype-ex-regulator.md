**Profile.** Retired from JFSA supervision or a BoJ examination function, typically 60s,
now holding one or two 顧問 or outside-director seats. Was in the room when the Principles
for Model Risk Management were drafted or when they were first examined against.

**What they buy us.** The single most valuable thing a validation boutique can claim:
that its work will survive supervisory scrutiny, asserted by someone who used to *be* the
scrutiny. Converts "is this vendor any good" into "this is what the examiner will look
for", which is the question the buyer actually has.

**What they cost.** Highest sensitivity to reputational risk of the three archetypes —
they will not attach their name to an unproven foreign-principal venture without a
credible academic and publication base first. This archetype must be approached *after*
the publishing stream has produced something, not before.

**Where they are found.** Post-retirement affiliations at universities and think-tanks;
advisory boards of Big 4 and domestic consultancies; JAFEE and academic conference
programmes; public panels on financial regulation.

**Risk.** Revolving-door optics and any live conflict with current supervisory duties must
be checked before approach. Confirm retirement status and any cooling-off obligations.
