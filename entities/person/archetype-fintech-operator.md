**New archetype, created 2026-09-22, and it follows directly from moving the target market
down-market.**

**Profile.** Has built or run a licensed lending, payments or insurance business in Japan —
貸金業 registration, a 少額短期保険 licence, or a fintech that got through JFSA's
registration process. Sits on the Fintech協会 board or its committees, mentors at FINOLAB,
judges at FINOPITCH. 40s–50s rather than 60s–70s. Possibly a serial founder or now at a VC.

**Why this displaces the ex-megabank CRO as first target.** A retired megabank CRO opens
megabank doors — which is the market we have just concluded is the *hardest and last* to
enter. They have no particular reach into a 30-person alternative lender, and their
credibility is calibrated to an audience we are not yet selling to. **Match the 顧問 to the
buyer tier, not to the prestige ladder.**

**What they buy us.** Direct access to founders in the startup and SME tiers, where a warm
introduction is a message rather than a procurement process. Practical knowledge of what
these firms are actually required to do and what their funding counterparties ask for — the
core of `note-sme-wedge`. And credibility with the people who fund them: VCs and warehouse
lenders.

**What they cost.** Materially less than a megabank CRO, and far more likely to accept
equity, a success fee or a reciprocal arrangement rather than a ¥300k monthly retainer. They
are also still commercially active, which cuts both ways — more useful, less available.

**Where they are found.** Fintech協会 member and board listings, FINOPITCH and Tokyo
Financial Award judging panels, FINOLAB, FIN/SUM speaker lists, and 顧問バンク.

**Do not discard the senior archetypes.** `person-archetype-ex-cro` and
`person-archetype-ex-regulator` remain the right profiles for the large and mega tiers —
they are simply a year-two relationship, approached once there is a reference case.
