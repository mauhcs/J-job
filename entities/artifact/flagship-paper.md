**Working thesis.** JFSA (AI Discussion Paper v1.0/v1.1), MAS (AI Risk Management
Guidelines, final expected mid-2026 with a 12-month compliance window) and the BoJ's 2026
generative-AI annex all now require *independent validation* of AI-driven models. None of
them specifies what that validation should consist of. The existing validation playbook —
built for parametric credit and market risk models — does not transfer cleanly to models
that are non-stationary, opaque, and retrained continuously.

The paper proposes a concrete validation protocol for this class, grounded in what
actually breaks: distribution shift, data quality and training-set sufficiency (both named
explicitly in the BoJ survey), explainability requirements in credit decisioning, and
backtesting under regime change.

**Why this piece and not another.** It sits at the exact intersection of the three streams.
It is a genuine methodological contribution (publishing), it is the topic every buyer in
all three geographies is currently under pressure on (market), and it is a natural
co-authorship proposal to an academic 顧問 candidate (advisor). One piece of work, three
returns.

**Route.** arXiv preprint → Journal of Risk Model Validation submission → JAFEE
presentation → practitioner talk at CFA Society Japan.

**Before writing:** the state-of-the-art survey in `stream-publishing` leg 2 has to happen
first, to confirm this is actually an open gap and not a crowded one.
