The megabanks are the institutions JFSA's Principles were written for, and they responded
by building internal model risk management functions. They are therefore **not** buyers of
a validation capability — they have one.

What they can buy is **independence and capacity at the margin**: an outside opinion on a
model their own team built, overflow during a regulatory exam cycle, or specialist review
of a model class their team lacks depth in (microstructure, execution, exotic pricing, and
increasingly AI/ML).

**Realistic assessment:** these are the slowest, most procurement-heavy, most
relationship-gated buyers in the market, and they are precisely where the 顧問 is
non-optional. Treat them as a year-two target that the publishing stream warms up, not as
the beachhead.

Confidence is `low` because nothing here is sourced yet — it is an inference from the
regulatory history, not a finding. Segment cards should be split into named institutions
once the next research cycle confirms who runs MRM and at what scale.
