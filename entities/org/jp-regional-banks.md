**The most promising buyer segment identified in this sweep**, and the one that most
deserves the next research cycle.

The BoJ's August 2026 annex reports that regional banks showed a **particularly notable
rise in AI adoption over the past year**, expanding from general administrative tasks into
**core operations using customer information** — while institutions broadly identify
governance and third-party risk management as the areas still needing improvement, and
around half report problems with training-data sufficiency and quality control.

A regional bank running AI in core customer-facing operations is squarely inside JFSA's
model risk expectations and the AI Discussion Paper's scope. Unlike a megabank it has no
standing MRM team of quants, and unlike a megabank it cannot absorb a Big 4 fee for a
governance transformation programme.

**That is the gap:** buyers under real regulatory pressure, with no internal capability,
priced out of the incumbent solution. They do not need a framework; they need someone
competent to validate a handful of material models, credibly and affordably, and sign it.

## Open questions

- How many regional banks, and which, have live AI in credit or customer-facing decisions?
- Do they procure this individually or through their IT vendors / centre organisations?
- What can they actually pay?
