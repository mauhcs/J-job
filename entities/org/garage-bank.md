**The warmest lead in the repo — you already worked here.**

The operating company is **ガレージバンク株式会社**, founded 22 January 2020, mission
「モノの価値を、みんなの『できる』に。」 cashari is its product; the company is the counterparty.

Garage Bank operates cashari, a leaseback app: users monetise physical assets (phones,
game consoles, brand goods, PCs, cameras, watches, cars) while continuing to use them,
with a three-month usage period after which they repurchase, extend, or release the item.

**The modelling angle is unusually favourable to us.** cashari deliberately underwrites on
*the value of the thing itself* rather than conventional credit criteria — that is how it
serves people who fail standard lending tests. So the model is a **residual-value and
recovery model**, not a credit scorecard. There is no standard validation playbook for it,
no Big 4 methodology to buy off the shelf, and the failure modes (secondhand price decay,
category concentration, adverse selection on repurchase, recovery rates on release) are
exactly the kind of thing an HFT-trained modeller reads naturally.

Recognition: **JETRO Award at FINOPITCH 2025** and **Audience Award at the Tokyo Financial
Awards 2024** — which also makes those two award programmes public target lists of
comparable companies (`org-tokyo-financial-award`).

**Why it matters beyond the engagement.** A startup at this size will not pay much. What it
can give is the **first reference case** — a completed external model validation with a
named counterparty, which is the thing every larger buyer and every 顧問 will ask to see
and which nothing else on this list can provide faster.

## Next action

An informal conversation, not a pitch: what model do they run now, who has ever reviewed
it, and has a funding counterparty or investor ever asked. That last question is the one
that tests the whole SME thesis (`note-sme-wedge`).
