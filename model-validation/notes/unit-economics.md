**Confidence is low on purpose.** No published pricing exists for model validation
engagements in Japan — consultancies quote bespoke and publish nothing. Everything below is
triangulated from adjacent markets. It is a planning frame, not a finding, and
`task-pricing-comparables` exists to replace it with something real.

## The two reference rates, and the gap between them

- **Contractor / staff augmentation.** Median day rate for a *Quantitative Risk Consultant*
  in London: **£215/day** (ITJobsWatch, six months to Dec 2025). This is the body-shop rate —
  a bank renting a pair of hands into its own team.
- **Niche independent expert.** General consulting benchmarks put specialist experts at
  **$300–500+/hour**, with day rates typically 6–8× the hourly rate.

That is roughly a **ten-fold gap for the same technical skill**, and which side of it you
land on is decided entirely by positioning, not by competence. Being rented as capacity puts
you at the bottom. Being engaged as the independent party whose signature means something —
academic affiliation, published methodology, named principal — puts you at the top.

This is the single most important economic fact in the plan: the whole publishing stream and
the academic affiliation are not vanity, they are **the mechanism that moves you across that
gap**, and they are worth roughly an order of magnitude on the same hours.

## A working rate

Plan against **¥300,000–500,000 per day** (roughly $2,000–3,300) for senior independent
specialist work. Below ¥200k/day you are in the staff-augmentation market and should decline.

## Capacity, honestly

Published benchmarks for solo consultants: **50–65% realistic utilisation**, about **25–30
billable hours a week** against ~52 hours worked, with the remainder going to business
development, marketing, admin and professional development. Small firms of 2–10 target
65–75%; below 55% is classified as struggling.

**A solo operator's ceiling is therefore ~150 billable days a year at full commitment** —
and that is for someone doing this as their whole job, which is not the situation here.

These are utilisation benchmarks for consultants **who already have a pipeline**. They say
nothing about how long it takes to acquire one, which is the actual binding constraint here.
See `note-sales-routes`.

## The arithmetic you were worried about, done

| commitment | billable days/yr | at ¥400k/day |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time solo, healthy utilisation | 150 | ¥60m |
| Full-time solo, realistic first two years | 90–110 | ¥36–44m |
| **Two days a week alongside IPOR and TUJ** | **70–80** | **¥28–32m** |
| One day a week | 35–40 | ¥14–16m |

**The concern is correct**: full-time solo at 150 billable days leaves essentially nothing
for business development, marketing and publishing — the 50–65% utilisation figure already
*assumes* those consume a third to a half of the week. You cannot bill 150 days and also do
BD at four tiers and write papers. The arithmetic simply does not close.

But it does not need to. See `note-feasible-play`.

## Engagement sizing — WITHDRAWN

This section carried a table of days and fees per tier. **It was fabricated** — a triangulated
day rate multiplied by a guessed day count, neither sourced. See
`note-correction-invented-numbers`.

It cannot be replaced by a better guess. Effort is the missing factor and no published data on
validation duration exists (`note-deliverable-spec`), so the number has to be *observed*:
`task-measure-real-validation-effort`. **Until then, quote nothing.**

What can be said without inventing anything: understanding someone else's model and getting
usable data are the dominant costs, and **they do not scale down with the client's budget**.
A ten-person lender's model can take as long to understand as a bank's, and is usually worse
documented. That alone is enough to doubt the startup tier — the effort floor is set by the
model, not by what the buyer can pay.

## What would change these numbers

- Whether Japanese buyers procure this as consulting, audit, or an outsourced function —
  audit-adjacent work prices higher and recurs annually.
- Whether a foreign principal can be validator of record without a domestic entity.
- Whether the first engagement can be repeated: the second validation of the same model class
  should take half the days of the first, and that is where the margin is.
