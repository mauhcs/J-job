Japan has a large, mature, commoditised market for renting senior corporate experience.
It is bigger than expected and it is generalist.

## Two structures, and they suit different problems

**エージェント型 (agent).** You brief a consultant on your 経営課題; they assign a match
from their pool. 顧問名鑑, HiPro Biz, ProShare (Pasona JOB HUB), プロシェアリング
(Circulation), マイナビ顧問. Better match quality, more process, built for companies that
can describe a business problem and pay for its solution.

**プラットフォーム型 (self-serve).** You search profiles and make contact yourself.
顧問バンク, クラウドリンクス. Cheaper, faster, and — the part that matters here — it lets
you *look at the pool before committing to anything*.

## Scale

| service | pool | evidence of throughput |
|---|---|---|
| クラウドリンクス | 128,000 | high-class talent not in the job market |
| 顧問名鑑 | 43,000+ | 38,393+ contracts, 7,000+ companies, since 2009 |
| HiPro Biz | 39,302 | 4,820 companies, 69 industries |
| プロシェアリング | 33,999 | 24,772 projects |
| ProShare (Pasona) | 15,000+ | placement in ~1 week |
| 顧問バンク | 10,000 | 55.9% listed-company officers |

## What it costs

The published numbers cluster tightly, so plan against them:

- プロフェッショナル人材バンク: **¥200,000+/month**, ¥200k–1,000,000/month by domain
- メンタープロパートナーズ: **¥250,000–350,000/month**
- Recruit Executive Agent: **35% success fee + ¥1,100,000**
- 顧問名鑑: free to search and meet; fees on contract; **one-year minimum 業務委託 term**
- 顧問バンク: ¥0 trial, **no intermediary fee** — the company pays the advisor directly,
  and 成果報酬 (success-fee) structures are supported

**Read: a Japanese 顧問 retainer is roughly ¥200,000–350,000 per month.** That is the
number the advisor stream has to plan against, and for a pre-revenue boutique it is the
constraint that shapes everything — which is why 顧問バンク's success-fee structure is more
interesting than its size.

## The important caveat

The comparison research explicitly notes that **none of these services advertises
finance/banking/securities ex-executives as a speciality.** They are generalist pools.
顧問名鑑 is the one whose registrant base is specifically described as including megabank
and securities-firm alumni, which is why it is P1 among the agent-type options.

So: the pool is large and cheap to search, but the hit rate on our actual profile — a
former head of model risk, or a retired supervisor — is unknown and probably low. These
platforms are a *sourcing channel of unproven yield*, not the answer to the advisor stream.

## The inversion, which is the real finding

The problem framed as "pay ¥300k/month to find someone who opens doors" has a cheaper
inverse: **register as an expert on VisasQ and get paid to have the conversations that
reveal where the doors are.**

VisasQ has 500,000 experts, offices in Tokyo, Hong Kong and Singapore — this plan's exact
geography — and pays a few hundred USD per hour. Japanese and Asian financial institutions
book experts to ask about quant modelling, risk and digital assets. Each booking is paid
primary research into what institutions are worried about and who is asking, it is
inbound rather than cold, and the role being sold — independent external expert — is the
product itself, in one-hour units.

It does not replace the 顧問. It removes the dependency of *market research* on finding one,
which is what is currently blocking three of the top questions in `note-open-questions`.
