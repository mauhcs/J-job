Hong Kong's requirements sit in the HKMA Supervisory Policy Manual, principally **CA-G-4
(Validating Risk Rating Systems)** and **CA-G-3 (Use of Internal Models Approach)**,
following Basel Committee standards for model development, validation and ongoing
monitoring across market, credit, liquidity and emerging risk.

**The clause to build on.** CA-G-4 contemplates that independent review or audit of an
authorized institution's rating systems *may be conducted by independent external parties
which have the relevant experience and knowledge of doing so*.

This is worth more than any marketing claim we could make. It means external validation in
Hong Kong is not a workaround a bank has to justify to its supervisor — it is a route the
supervisor has already written down. Quote it verbatim in the first deck.

Two further expectations from the PwC HK 2024 paper, both useful as sales hooks: validation
**should not be limited to back-testing**, and institutions must maintain a **model
inventory** with scope, materiality and methodology per model.

**Strategic implication.** Hong Kong is likely the faster first sale: explicit regulatory
permission, English-language contracting, shorter procurement cycles. Japan remains the
strategic home market. Sequencing HK-first also produces the reference case that makes the
Japanese conversation possible — which the 顧問 will ask for.
