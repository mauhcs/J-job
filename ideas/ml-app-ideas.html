New, unusual, or worth knowing

  • Text-to-3D (~140 models) and Image-to-3D (~783) are the thinnest categories on the site — early enough that a working mobile pipeline would be unusual.
  • Any-to-Any (~11,700 models) is now one of the largest tags and is where omni-modal models land. A grab-bag rather than a well-defined task.
  • Visual Document Retrieval is the ColPali idea — embed page images instead of extracted text. Directly useful for anything ingesting statements or filings.
  • Time-series forecasting is not on the /tasks page at all, but ?pipeline_tag=time-series-forecasting is a live model filter with Chronos, TimesFM and Granite TTM under it. The catalog page and the model filters do not fully agree.
  • Graph neural networks have no task tag. For the wallet-scam idea on your list you are on PyTorch Geometric or DGL, not the Hub.
  • granite-timeseries-ttm-r2 is ~1M parameters — genuinely phone-sized forecasting, which matters for the funding-rate app already on your list.

02Why these eight

Your existing list already covers time-series forecasting, VLM document scanning, Whisper voice logging, RL market-making, LLM negotiation, object detection on a poker table, and GNNs for wallet risk. So these deliberately use the task families you have not touched: audio classification, sentence similarity and ranking, video classification, summarization, mask generation, keypoint detection, zero-shot detection and tabular inference — plus one RL idea that is a different game from market-making.

3.1VoiceGuard

Live scam-call screener.

audio-classificationASR text-classification on-device — and must be difficulty 4

Started when an unknown caller claims to be from a bank or exchange. The app streams the call through two models at once: one asks is this voice synthetic?, the other transcribes and asks is this a known scam script? A bar fills as evidence accumulates, and it surfaces the phrases that triggered it — "remote access", "transfer to a safe account", "read me the seed phrase".

Data

ASVspoof2019_LA and ASVspoof2021_DF are the standard anti-spoofing corpora, both with real download traffic. garystafford/deepfake-audio-detection is a smaller convenience set. The scam-script classifier you build: scrape published 警察庁 / FCA / FTC transcripts, then synthesise variants with a small LLM.

Runs where

On-device, and it has to be — streaming call audio to a server is the wrong design for a privacy-sensitive fraud tool. A 95M audio classifier quantises under 100 MB; Whisper runs via Core ML (whisper.cpp) or ONNX Runtime Mobile, starting from onnx-community/whisper-tiny.en.

Skills practised

Fine-tuning an audio spectrogram transformer; streaming inference with hysteresis so the score doesn't flicker; calibration — accusing a real bank is the costly error; int8 quantization; two models under one latency budget.

Weekend one

Drop live calls entirely. Ten fake and ten real clips, fine-tune the AST spoof detector on ASVspoof, one screen that plays a clip and shows a score. Audio capture is the hard half — defer it.

3.2Déjà Vu

Token whitepaper plagiarism detector.

sentence-similaritytext-ranking zero-shot-classification fully on-device difficulty 2

Paste a URL or PDF for a token you're considering. The app embeds it section by section, compares against a local index of known rug-pull and template whitepapers, and returns nearest neighbours with matching passages side by side — "this tokenomics section is 0.94 cosine to three 2021 rugs."

Models

ModelSizeLicence
sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2~22Mapache-2.0
BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.533Mmit
MoritzLaurer/deberta-v3-base-zeroshot-v2.0184Mmit

Data

No whitepaper corpus exists on the Hub — I searched and found nothing usable. You build it: scrape whitepapers for tokens flagged on rug-pull trackers, plus a control set of survivors. A few hundred documents is enough for retrieval, and labelling is coarse (rugged / alive) and comes free from the tracker.

Runs where

Entirely on-device. 22M embeddings run via Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (ONNX) or a Core ML export; the index is a few megabytes of float16.

Skills practised

Embedding models and vector search; contrastive fine-tuning with MultipleNegativesRankingLoss to pull template clones together; two-stage retrieval — bi-encoder recall then cross-encoder rerank; evaluating retrieval honestly with recall@k rather than accuracy.

Weekend one

50 whitepapers, MiniLM embeddings, plain numpy cosine, one screen showing top-5 neighbours with matched paragraphs. No fine-tuning yet.

3.3Grade It

Trading-card condition grader.

mask-generationsegmentation keypoint-detectiondepth-estimation image-classification mostly on-device difficulty 4

Lay a card on a table; the app guides you to a square-on shot, identifies the card, then measures what graders measure: centering from border widths off segmentation masks, corners and edges from classified crops, and surface scratches with a depth map as a side channel. Output is a predicted grade with the per-criterion measurements behind it.

Models

ModelSizeLicence
facebook/sam-vit-base94Mapache-2.0
google/vit-base-patch16-224 — fine-tune for corner wear86Mapache-2.0
openai/clip-vit-base-patch32 — zero-shot card ID~151Mnot stated

Data

There is no card-grading dataset on the Hub — I searched card grading and got nothing. Card images exist in small community sets like netprtony/pokemon-cards-image-and-annotations and tooni/pokemoncards, but none carry condition labels. You create it, and that's the interesting part: photograph your own cards, and get labels free by scraping completed listings of already-graded slabs, where the grade is printed on the label.

Runs where

Mostly on-device. SAM-base is borderline but mobile variants exist; depth has a verified Core ML export at apple/coreml-depth-anything-small and ONNX at onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small. The classifier heads are tiny.

Skills practised

Multi-task learning (four criteria, one backbone); turning a mask into a geometric measurement rather than a label, a different discipline from classification; ordinal regression — grades 1–10 are ordered, and treating them as unordered classes throws away structure; capture-quality gating, since a bad photo should be rejected rather than scored.

Weekend one

Centering only. SAM to mask the card and the inner border, compute left/right and top/bottom ratios, show the numbers. No grade prediction, no learning — just the measurement, which is already useful.

3.4Pull Audit

Gacha odds auditor.

document-QAimage-text-to-text table-QAtabular on-device with SmolVLM difficulty 3

Photograph a game's published drop-rate table; a VLM parses it into structured rates. Then log pulls — or screenshot results and let the model read them. The app maintains a Bayesian posterior over the true rate and tells you when your observed pulls are genuinely inconsistent with the disclosure, with a sequential test that doesn't lie about multiple looks at the data.

Models

ModelSizeLicence
HuggingFaceTB/SmolVLM-256M-Instruct256Mapache-2.0
Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct3.75Bnot stated
impira/layoutlm-document-qa~128Mmit
microsoft/tapex-base-finetuned-wtq139Mmit

Data

Your own, and easy: screenshot real gacha rate tables — they're published because JOGA/CESA guidelines require it — and hand-label fifty into JSON. For the statistics you need no data at all: simulate pulls with pity timers and ceilings and check your test's false-positive rate against ground truth you control.

Runs where

SmolVLM-256M is genuinely phone-sized. Qwen2.5-VL-3B needs a server or a high-end device with aggressive quantization. The statistics are arithmetic.

Skills practised

Structured extraction from a VLM — constrained decoding into JSON, badly underrated as a skill; evaluating extraction by field-level accuracy, not BLEU; and the honest statistics of sequential testing, where the fun is proving to yourself that "80 pulls and nothing, the rates must be fake" usually isn't significant.

Weekend one

Skip the camera. Type a rate and your results, show the posterior with a credible interval and a sequential-test verdict. The interesting part is the statistics, and it needs no ML to start.

3.5Tell

Gameplay cheat spotter.

video-classificationkeypoint-detection object-detection needs a server non-commercial models difficulty 4

Share a suspicious clip from a match. The app classifies short windows for inhuman aim behaviour — snap-to-target, zero overshoot, inhuman reaction latency — and returns a timeline of flagged moments with the motion traces drawn over the frames, rather than a bare verdict.

Models

ModelSizeLicence
MCG-NJU/videomae-base-finetuned-kinetics87Mcc-by-nc-4.0
facebook/timesformer-base-finetuned-k400not reportedcc-by-nc-4.0
hustvl/yolos-tiny — crosshair tracking6Mapache-2.0

Data

Yours, and you can generate the positives: play with a legitimate assist setting cranked up, or script mouse input to produce synthetic aimbot traces, against your own normal play as negatives. taesiri/GameplayVideoSplits exists but is tiny.

Runs where

Server, realistically — video transformers over a clip aren't a phone workload. The phone records and uploads. A tracking-only version with YOLOS-tiny could run locally.

Skills practised

Temporal modelling and clip sampling; the derivative trick — the signal isn't in the pixels but in the trajectory of the crosshair, so extracting motion first and classifying that is cheaper and more accurate than a video transformer; extreme class imbalance; adversarial thinking, since cheats now deliberately add human-like jitter.

Weekend one

No video model. Track the crosshair with YOLOS-tiny, extract angular velocity and acceleration, plot your own traces next to a scripted snap. If those separate visually the project is viable; if not, you've saved weeks.

3.6Proposal Pulse

DAO governance reader and vote predictor.

summarizationzero-shot-classification token-classificationtabular summariser needs a server difficulty 3

A feed of live governance proposals across the DAOs you hold tokens in. Each gets a three-line summary, a classification of what it actually does — treasury spend, parameter change, upgrade, social — a flag for anything touching your position, and a predicted outcome from how the large delegates have voted historically.

Data

No usable governance corpus on the Hub — the only hit was achiepatricia/han-governance-proposal-dataset-v1 with 17 downloads, which isn't something to build on. Pull it from the Snapshot and Tally public APIs: proposal text, choices, final tallies, per-delegate votes. The labels are free and already in the data — outcomes are recorded — which makes this the cleanest supervised setup on the list.

Runs where

The vote predictor is a small tabular model and runs anywhere. BART-large summarization needs a server; a LoRA-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B is the on-device route and a good exercise in itself.

Skills practised

LoRA / PEFT fine-tuning for domain summarization — the single most transferable skill here; measuring summarization honestly (humans or an LLM judge, not ROUGE alone); combining a text model with a tabular one; and the forecasting discipline you already like, since a vote predictor is a calibrated binary forecaster and deserves a Brier score.

Weekend one

Vote prediction only, no NLP. 500 closed Snapshot proposals, features = top-10 delegate voting history plus quorum and proposal type, gradient boosting, report Brier against the base rate. That tells you whether governance is predictable before you touch summaries.

3.7Last Look

RL auction trainer.

reinforcement-learning policy is tens of KB — fully on-device difficulty 3

Bid against trained agents across a sequence of auctions — NFT drops, collectible lots, liquidations — with the format switching between English, sealed-bid and Dutch. Afterwards the app decomposes your loss: how much from the winner's curse, how much from bidding into a thin book, how much from simply overpaying. Agents are labelled by policy so you learn which behaviour beat you.

Models

Nothing off-the-shelf fits — auction environments aren't a standard Hub benchmark and I won't pretend otherwise. You train your own with Stable-Baselines3 as reference; sb3/ppo-LunarLander-v2 exists and is useful only as a template for packaging a trained policy to the Hub.

Data

None needed — the environment is the data, which is the appeal. Write a Gymnasium env with configurable private-value distributions and bidder counts. Optionally calibrate the value distribution to real OpenSea or Blur sale histories so it feels like the market rather than a textbook.

Runs where

Entirely on-device. A trained PPO policy for a low-dimensional auction is tens of kilobytes as ONNX or Core ML, with no server at all.

Skills practised

The full RL loop: environment design — the hard part, and the part tutorials skip — reward shaping, PPO, self-play. And the thing that makes this finance rather than games: checking your agent against the known analytic equilibrium. First-price sealed-bid with uniform private values has a closed-form optimal bid, so you can verify your agent actually converged instead of merely looking busy. A rare luxury in RL.

Weekend one

One format — second-price sealed bid, three bidders, uniform values. Train PPO, plot the learned bid function against the analytic one. If they coincide, the scaffolding works.

3.8Level Up

Photo to playable level.

mask-generationsegmentation depth-estimationzero-shot-detection on-device, and should be zero training data difficulty 2

Photograph anything — a desk, a street, a bookshelf. The app segments it into solid regions, turns the masks into collision geometry, and drops a character in. You play a platformer built out of your own surroundings, then share the level as a photo plus a seed.

On-device, and it should be: verified Core ML (apple/coreml-depth-anything-small) and ONNX (onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small) exports exist for depth, and SAM has mobile variants. Grounding DINO is the heaviest piece and is optional.

Weekend one

Still images, no game. Tap a photo, SAM segments the region, draw the mask outline as a polygon. If the polygons look like level geometry, continue; if they look like noise, you've learned something cheap.

04Top three

First

Grade It

Because the data is the exercise. Getting labels by scraping already-graded slabs is a weak-supervision trick you'll reuse for the rest of your career, and the problem forces three things finance people are usually weak at: measurement from masks rather than classification, ordinal targets, and input-quality gating. Graded cards also have public comps, so your model output has a verifiable dollar value — that closes the loop in a way a game doesn't.

Second

VoiceGuard

The hardest and the most worth doing. Genuinely useful, has to be on-device for reasons you can articulate, and the only idea here where calibration is the whole product — a scam detector that cries wolf gets uninstalled. Same discipline as model risk: the costs of a false positive and a false negative differ and you must say so. It also runs three modalities under a latency budget, which is the most realistic engineering on the list.

Third

Last Look

Pick it precisely because there's no dataset and no pretrained model — you build the environment, train the agent, then check it against a closed-form answer. Almost nothing in deep learning lets you verify convergence against known truth, and an auction game does. It'll also teach you something real about bidding that the deep-hedging game won't: hedging is about variance, auctions are about adverse selection.

Honourable mention: Déjà Vu is the easiest thing here and the fastest route to something you'd actually show someone. If you want a win this weekend rather than a project, start there.

05Caveats

  • Sizes and download counts were read from the Hub API on 2026-10-13. Sizes come from safetensors.total; where the API didn't report one I've said so rather than guessed.
  • Three licence situations to settle before shipping anything. Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and openai/clip-vit-base-patch32 state no licence in their card metadata, and both video backbones in 3.5 are cc-by-nc-4.0 — non-commercial.
  • ProsusAI/finbert is cc-by-nc-sa-4.0, which is why it isn't recommended above despite being the obvious finance sentiment model.
  • Datasets I could not find, stated plainly: no card-grading corpus, no token-whitepaper corpus, no usable DAO-governance corpus, no aimbot corpus. In all four cases building the dataset is the most valuable part of the project.
